## Quality

The worry is fair. What the listings say:

- **Mostly MDF.** Only pine slats and some frame parts are solid; the
  sellers' "wood" means MDF. MDF is heavy and fine on a flat floor but
  doesn't take repeated assembly and its screw holes strip.
- **No reviews anywhere** for the UK version.
- **Four parcels, 99 kg, flat-pack** with mains sockets built in.
- In its favour: 200 kg load, the same design in many shops (so the parts
  work), and a price that's low for the size.

If buying: get it from a seller with a returns address in the UK (B&Q
marketplace gives B&Q's buyer protection), check the socket strip has a UKCA
or CE mark, and fix the bookcase to the wall.

### Build it from parts

Likely the best quality for the money: a plain daybed plus fixed
bookcases and a desk top, each from a maker with a known record.

- IKEA daybed (HEMNES or BRIMNES) with BILLY bookcases along the side
  and foot, fixed to the wall, and a worktop on a BILLY or legs for the
  desk. Someone has built a daybed nook from four BILLYs on a timber base
  ([IKEA Hackers](https://ikeahackers.net/2018/03/daybed-reading-nook-ikea-bookshelves.html)),
  and a desk shelf in a BILLY ([IKEA Hackers](https://ikeahackers.net/2011/04/billy-desk.html)).
- Or a joiner builds it in, which gives exactly the room's sizes.
